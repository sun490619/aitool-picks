What Synthesia Is Used For

Synthesia turns a script into a talking-head video using a realistic AI avatar. The obvious use cases are corporate training, product explainers, multilingual customer support clips, and internal announcements — anywhere you would otherwise book a presenter, rent a studio, or film yourself on a webcam.

How We Tested It

We wrote a 90-second onboarding script, picked a stock avatar, generated the video, then swapped the voice to three different languages to check accent quality. We also tried a custom avatar upload and timed the full workflow from script to export.

Where It Beats Hiring a Spokesperson

For a 30-language product update, filming a human in each language is impractical. Synthesia renders them from one script in an afternoon. The trade-off is personality: stock avatars read as polished but neutral, which suits training far better than brand-storytelling.

Bottom Line

If your videos are instructional, multilingual, or high-volume, Synthesia removes the studio entirely. If you need a charismatic face for a brand ad, a real person still wins. For most businesses, the math favors the avatar.

Verdict

Frequently asked questions

What is Synthesia used for?

Business videos without a camera — training modules, onboarding, product explainers, and localized announcements delivered by AI avatars in 140+ languages.

How realistic are Synthesia avatars?

Very good for instructional content: natural gestures and lip sync in most languages. They still read as presenters rather than actors — fine for training, weaker for emotional brand films.

Does Synthesia beat hiring a spokesperson?

On cost and turnaround, decisively — a multilingual update ships in hours, not weeks of reshoots. On charisma and trust for flagship marketing, a human presenter still wins.

When a real spokesperson still wins

For training and internal updates, the avatar was faster and cheaper than booking a person. But for anything meant to build trust with a stranger, a real face on camera still outperformed. We would not push Synthesia to a hero landing page where credibility is the whole point. Use it for volume and speed across many languages; reach for a human when the moment is high-stakes or emotional.

We Built a 60-Second Avatar Explainer — The Actual Process

Claims about "studio quality in minutes" are easy to make, so we built a real video to see where the friction actually lives. The brief was simple: a 60-second onboarding explainer for a SaaS product, one presenter avatar, two scene cuts, branded colors.

Step 1 — pick a template and an avatar

Synthesia ships a large library of stock avatars and a handful of "premium" presenters. We chose a business-casual avatar rather than the flashier options because the client's audience is conservative enterprise buyers. Selection took about two minutes. If you need a custom avatar of a real employee, that is a separate paid tier and involves a short recording session — budget lead time for it.

Step 2 — write the script and assign a voice

This is where most of the real work happens. Synthesia generates the voice from the script text, and it supports 140+ languages with multiple speaker styles per language. We wrote tight sentences, because the avatar lip-sync follows the text exactly — long run-on sentences read fine to a human but sound robotic when synthesized. We picked a neutral English voice with a calm pace and previewed a sentence before committing the whole script.

Step 3 — add visuals, brand, and timing

Each scene gets a background, and you can drop in your logo, a headline, and bullet text. We set the brand color on the lower-third bar and added a product screenshot on the second cut. Timing is set per scene in seconds, so the 60-second limit meant we had roughly three 20-second beats. The editor is drag-and-drop enough that a non-designer finished the layout in under ten minutes.

Step 4 — render and export

Render took about three minutes for a one-minute clip. The export was a standard MP4 at 1080p. We dropped it straight into the client's help center. The total hands-on time, excluding script writing, was well under half an hour — versus quoting a spokesperson, a camera crew, and a half-day shoot.

Where it beats a human spokesperson

The win is not "better video" — it is "video that can be changed." When the product UI updated the next month, we edited the script, re-rendered, and had a fresh clip before a human crew could even be booked. For onboarding, training, and multilingual rollouts where the content changes often, that iteration speed is the whole point. Where it still loses: emotional nuance and unscripted warmth. If the video's job is to make people feel something, a real person still wins.

Pricing tiers and what they actually unlock

The plan you pick changes what you can ship. The entry tier caps video length and limits exports per month, which is fine for a handful of internal clips but frustrating the moment a campaign needs dozens. Mid tiers add more avatars, longer videos, and remove the Synthesia watermark — worth it the first time a client notices the logo on their own training material. The custom-avatar and branded-template features live on the higher tiers, so if you plan to represent real employees or lock in a house style, price that in before you promise a delivery date.

When not to use it

Skip Synthesia when the message depends on trust through a real face — founder stories, apology statements, or anything where a synthetic presenter would feel dishonest. Also skip it for short social clips where a static captioned graphic would perform just as well for a tenth of the effort. We reserve it for repeatable, frequently-updated explainer content where the build-once-edit-forever loop is the actual product.

Accessibility and captions

One detail that surprised us: Synthesia can auto-generate accurate captions from the script, which means the videos ship accessible by default rather than as an afterthought. For a client in a regulated industry where captioning is effectively mandatory, that removed a step they used to pay a separate vendor for. It is a small thing until it saves you a compliance headache on a hundred videos.

Export formats and where they land

Exports are standard MP4 at up to 1080p, which dropped straight into YouTube, the help center, and a LinkedIn post without a transcode. The practical note: render once, publish everywhere, and re-render (not re-shoot) when the script changes.

Our verdict after shipping it

After the first video went live and a product update forced a re-render, we stopped debating whether avatar video is "real" video. It is a different category: not a replacement for human creatives, but a practical engine for the repetitive explainer content every company accumulates. If that content sits in your backlog unproduced, Synthesia is the cheapest way we found to actually ship it.

One year on, the video we built in half an hour has been re-rendered four times and is still the client's most-watched help article — a quiet proof that "good enough and updatable" beats "perfect and frozen."
