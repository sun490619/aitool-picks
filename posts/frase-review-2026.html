Frase is an AI content tool built for one job: helping you research, write, and optimize SEO articles without bouncing between five browser tabs. You hand it a target keyword, it pulls the current top-ranking Google results, analyzes what those pages actually cover, and spits out a structured content brief — headings, common questions, statistics, and the terms you'll need to rank. Then you draft inside Frase's own editor while a live "content score" tells you how close you are to matching the pages already on page one.

We spent two weeks running Frase on real client-style assignments: a 2,000-word "best backpacking tents" buyer's guide and a 1,200-word "what is programmatic SEO" explainer. Here's what actually happened, not the marketing version.

Quick Verdict

Bottom line: Frase does three things well — it auto-generates SERP-based briefs in under a minute, keeps a live content score as you draft, and bundles all of it into one editor at roughly half of Surfer SEO's price. The AI writing itself is competent but not best-in-class; treat it as a drafting aid, not a replacement for a human editor.

Best for: Solo bloggers, agency content teams, and SEO freelancers who publish multiple articles a week and hate the manual "open ten tabs, read competitors, build an outline" dance.

Key Features

1. Automated Content Briefs

Type a keyword like "best hiking backpacks" and Frase scans the current top-20 Google results, then extracts their H2/H3 structure, the topics they cover, and the sources they cite. Click "Generate Brief" and you get a ready outline with suggested headings in about 30 seconds. In our test it pulled 14 competitor headings and 9 "People also ask" questions we'd have missed doing it by hand.

2. SERP Analysis

The SERP view lays the ranking pages side by side with word counts and a topic map. You can read a competitor's full outline without leaving Frase, which cuts the research step from roughly 20 minutes to about 3. Data comes from Frase's own index; we found it accurate for high-volume keywords but occasionally stale for very fresh news topics.

3. AI Writer & Document Editor

The editor is a Google-Docs-style canvas with an outline builder on the left and an AI toolbar. "Write for me" expands a heading into a paragraph, and there are templates for intros, product descriptions, and listicles. Output is solid first-draft material but reads generically on complex subjects — we always rewrote the technical sections by hand.

4. Content Scorer (Frase Score)

As you write, Frase compares your draft against the terms top-ranking pages use and scores you 0–100. It surfaces a checklist of "missing topics" and "terms to add." It's a genuinely useful guardrail, though easy to game — stuffing every suggested keyword hits 100 but won't beat pages with real depth. Use it as a coverage checklist, not a number to max out.

5. Answer Engine & Integrations

Frase suggests questions pulled from "People also ask", Reddit, and Quora so you can build FAQ sections that match real search intent. Integrations cover Google Search Console (import your own query data), WordPress (publish directly), and Google Docs.

Brief Quality Test

We ran the same keyword through Frase and built a brief for "programmatic SEO." Frase returned a 22-item outline including subtopics (weighted SERPs, dynamic rendering, Zapier examples) that matched what the current top-three results actually covered. The brief saved us roughly 40 minutes of manual competitor reading. The one gap: it occasionally suggested a heading no top page used, so we trimmed two low-value sections before writing.

SERP Analysis Test

For a mid-volume keyword ("cold email templates") the SERP table matched live Google results on 9 of 10 positions. The outlier was a featured snippet Google rotates daily. Word-count figures were within about 5% of what we measured by hand. Good enough to plan around — not a replacement for a dedicated rank tracker.

Optimization in Practice

Our "best backpacking tents" draft started at a Frase score of 34. Adding the suggested "packed weight," "season rating," and "tent pole material" sections pushed it to 81 within a single editing pass — and those were legitimately useful things a buyer wants, not filler. That's the right way to use the scorer: let it remind you of coverage gaps, then write real sentences. Don't just chase the number.

Pros & Cons

✅ Pros

  • Brief generation is genuinely fast — under a minute for most keywords
  • All-in-one: research, writing, and optimization in a single tab
  • Roughly half the price of Surfer SEO's equivalent plan
  • Content score keeps you honest about topic coverage
  • 5-day money-back means low risk to test

❌ Cons

  • AI writing is average — fine for drafts, weak on nuanced or technical topics
  • SERP data can lag on breaking-news keywords
  • No built-in plagiarism or fact-checking layer
  • UI feels dated next to Surfer's polish; occasional lag on long documents
  • WordPress is the main CMS integration — other platforms need copy-paste

Final Verdict

Frase won't write your article for you, and the AI prose won't win any awards. What it does do — kill the research-and-outline tax that makes publishing feel slow — it does better than anything in its price range. If you write SEO content regularly and your bottleneck is "I don't know what to cover," start here. Pair it with a human edit pass and it's a real productivity multiplier.
