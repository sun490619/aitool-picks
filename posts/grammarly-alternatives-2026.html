Best Grammarly Alternatives in 2026

Maya Rivera2026-07-277 min read By Maya Rivera

The best Grammarly alternative depends on need: QuillBot for paraphrasing, ProWritingAid for deep reports, LanguageTool for privacy, and Hemingway for blunt clarity.

Why look for alternatives

Grammarly is a strong all-rounder, but it is not the right fit for everyone. Three patterns push people to look elsewhere. First, cost: Grammarly's full feature set sits behind a recurring subscription, and freelancers or students on a tight budget often prefer a one-time or free option. Second, focus: if your main need is paraphrasing or heavy style restructuring, a dedicated tool does it better than a general grammar checker. Third, privacy and language coverage: some writers handle sensitive documents and want a tool that can run with stronger data protections or supports more languages than Grammarly's core set.

The good news is that "the best Grammarly alternative" is not one tool — it is whichever one matches your actual workflow. Below are the four we recommend most often, each strong in a different dimension.

The best alternatives

QuillBot — best for paraphrasing

QuillBot started as a paraphrasing engine and that is still where it shines. Its rephrase modes (standard, fluency, creative, formal, shorten, and expand) let you rewrite a sentence while keeping its meaning, which is ideal for avoiding repetition or reshaping awkward phrasing. It also bundles a summarizer, a grammar checker, and a citation generator. The free tier is usable; the premium plan unlocks all modes and higher word limits. If your Grammarly complaint is "I just need to reword things," QuillBot is the natural first stop.

ProWritingAid — best for deep editing reports

ProWritingAid is built for people who want to understand why their writing works or doesn't. Instead of a single correctness score, it runs more than twenty reports covering pacing, sentence-length variety, sticky words, clichés, and dialogue tags. It integrates with Scrivener and Word, which makes it popular with novelists and long-form authors rather than quick email writers. It is heavier than Grammarly, but for revision-heavy work the depth is the point.

LanguageTool — best for privacy and many languages

LanguageTool takes a different stance: broad language support (more than twenty languages) and a privacy-first posture that can even run self-hosted. Its corrections are sometimes less aggressive than Grammarly's on English nuance, but for multilingual teams or anyone handling confidential text, that trade-off is worth it. There is a free tier and a premium plan for advanced style suggestions.

Hemingway Editor — best for blunt clarity

Hemingway does almost nothing Grammarly does, and that is the appeal. It scores your text for reading grade level, highlights long sentences, passive voice, and filler words, and pushes you toward shorter, bolder writing. It is a clarity tool, not a proofreader, so many writers use it as a final pass after a grammar checker rather than a replacement. The web editor is free; the desktop app is a one-time purchase.

How to choose

Start from your weakest point. If you rewrite constantly, pick QuillBot. If you want to learn to write better over time, pick ProWritingAid. If you write in multiple languages or care about data privacy, pick LanguageTool. If your drafts come out wordy, pick Hemingway Editor. Many people end up using two: a grammar checker plus one specialty tool.
