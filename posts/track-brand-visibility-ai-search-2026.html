How to Track and Improve Your Brand's Visibility in AI Answers (2026)

By Maya RiveraAI Writing & Productivity Editor

Updated July 2026 · Maya Rivera

When a customer asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "what's the best tool for X?", an answer appears — and it names a few brands. If yours is missing, you never even entered the shortlist. Unlike classic search, there is no ranking report to check. So the first job is simple to say and easy to skip: find out what AI is saying about you. Here is how to measure it, benchmark it, and improve it without a big budget.

Why AI visibility needs its own measurement

Google Search Console tells you where you rank for a keyword. It says nothing about whether an AI recommended you in a conversation the user never turned into a click. As more decisions start (and sometimes finish) inside an assistant, that blind spot grows. Measuring AI visibility means tracking two things: whether you appear in generated answers for the questions that matter, and how you are described when you do.

The free way to start today

You do not need software to begin. Make a short list of the 10–20 questions a buyer would ask before choosing something like yours ("best budget invoicing app for freelancers", "alternatives to [competitor]"). Then:

  • Ask each question in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews.
  • Record whether you are mentioned, in what position, and how accurately you are described.
  • Note which sources the AI cites — those pages are your real competition for the answer.
  • Repeat monthly and watch the trend.

This manual audit is unglamorous but honest, and it teaches you exactly which questions you are losing — the input every later step depends on.

Dedicated AI-visibility trackers

Once you want continuous monitoring across many prompts and engines, a category of "answer-engine tracking" tools now exists (Otterly.ai, Peec AI, Profound and others). They run your prompts on a schedule, log which brands each engine names, measure your "share of voice" against rivals, and flag the citations feeding those answers. If AI referrals already matter to your revenue, that automation saves hours of manual checking. Trial one before committing — the space is young and features change fast.

Watch your analytics for AI referrals

Cited answers do sometimes send clicks. In your analytics, filter referral traffic for domains such as perplexity.ai, chatgpt.com and gemini.google.com. The numbers are usually small today but growing, and a rising line is proof your GEO work is landing. Tag those sessions and watch what they do — AI-referred visitors often arrive with high intent because the assistant has already pre-qualified them.

Improving the content behind the answers

Measurement only pays off if it changes what you publish. When the audit shows a question you are losing, build the best possible page for it. Two tools we use here earn their place:

  • Frase — maps the sub-questions and competing pages around a topic, so your page covers the full "question cluster" an AI draws from.
  • NeuronWriter — grades your draft against what already ranks and answers well, closing the gaps that keep you out of the citation set.

Before anything ships, confidence matters: run key pages through Originality.ai so what you publish reads as genuinely human and trustworthy — exactly the signal answer engines reward.

Don't forget the technical foundation

An AI can only cite a page it can reach and read. Confirm the AI crawlers you want are not blocked with a quick robots.txt check, keep your content in the HTML, and describe it with clean structured data. For the full playbook on shaping the content itself, see our companion guide to Generative Engine Optimization.

A realistic cadence

You do not need to obsess daily. A workable rhythm: run the manual prompt audit once a month, watch AI referral traffic weekly, and rebuild one losing page per sprint. Small, steady improvement compounds — and because most competitors are not measuring this at all yet, consistency alone can put you ahead. Note too that assistants and their sources vary by country and language, so audit in the markets you actually serve.
