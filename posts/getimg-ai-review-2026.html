What is getimg.ai?

getimg.ai is an AI image platform that goes beyond a single text-to-image box. It bundles several models (including Stable Diffusion XL and other SD variants), a set of editing tools like inpainting and face restoration, and a developer API. The pitch is a one-stop workspace for generating, fixing, and shipping images without jumping between separate apps.

How it works

You pick a model and a mode — text-to-image, image-to-image, inpaint, outpaint, face restore, or AI filter — type a prompt (with optional negative prompt), set aspect ratio and steps, and generate. Results stream into a gallery where you can refine or download. Everything is credit-based: each action spends credits from your monthly allowance.

Key features

  • Multiple models — SDXL and other Stable Diffusion checkpoints, switchable per generation.
  • Inpainting & outpainting — erase part of an image and redraw it, or extend the canvas.
  • Face restoration — clean up old or low-res portraits.
  • AI filters — restyle an image into different looks.
  • Logo & vector tools — generate simple logos and SVGs.
  • REST API — plug generation and editing into your own product.

Our hands-on test

We prompted a product shot, then used inpainting to swap the background and face restore on a soft portrait. The base generations were solid for social and blog use, and inpainting behaved predictably when the mask was clean. On very busy masks it occasionally leaked edges, so precise masking matters. Generation speed on a paid plan was fast enough for batch work. Overall it felt like a genuine studio toolkit, not a toy.

Pricing

getimg.ai gives free monthly credits, enough to try generation and edits. Paid plans add more credits, faster queues, and higher resolution. Prices move, so check the pricing page, but the entry paid tier is modest — reasonable for a freelancer or small team shipping regular visuals.

Who should use it

getimg.ai suits designers, content creators, and marketers who need both fresh images and quick edits without a Photoshop marathon. Developers can wire the API into apps. It is less ideal if you only want one hero image once in a while — then a simpler generator is enough — or if you need strict, license-clean commercial rights, which you should confirm per plan.

What we liked

  • Generation and editing live in one workspace.
  • Inpainting and face restore actually save time.
  • Free credits make a real trial possible.
  • API opens up product integrations.

What we didn’t

  • Messy masks can leak edges on edits.
  • Credit metering means heavy users watch the balance.
  • Commercial rights vary by plan — verify before reselling.

Pros & cons

In short: a practical all-in-one image toolkit with strong editing, as long as you mask carefully and track credits.

Verdict

If your work mixes creating and fixing images, getimg.ai removes a lot of app-switching. Start on free credits, learn masking, and step up to a paid plan once the volume justifies it. For pure one-off hero images, a lighter tool may do — but getimg.ai earns its place for ongoing visual work.

FAQ

What models does getimg.ai use?

getimg.ai runs several models including Stable Diffusion XL and other Stable Diffusion variants, plus style presets. You can switch models per generation and fine-tune with prompts and negative prompts.

Can I edit existing images, not just generate?

Yes. Beyond text-to-image, getimg.ai offers image-to-image, inpainting (erase and redraw part of an image), outpainting (extend a canvas), face restoration, and AI filters.

Is there a free tier?

Yes. getimg.ai gives free monthly credits so you can try generations and edits without paying. Paid plans add more credits and faster queues.

Is there an API?

Yes. getimg.ai provides a REST API so developers can plug image generation and editing into their own apps and workflows.

We signed up, generated a set of images across prompts, then ran inpainting, outpainting, and face restore on real samples. We judged output quality, edit predictability, speed on a paid plan, and how the credit system felt in practice. No vendor paid for a ranking.
